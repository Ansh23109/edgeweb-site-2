Most agency pitches sound similar once you've heard three of them. "We're a full-service team." "We prioritize communication." "We've delivered 100+ projects." None of that tells you anything, because none of it is falsifiable in a 30-minute call.

These eight questions are the ones that actually separate a good agency from a good pitch — because the answers are specific, checkable, and hard to fake.

1. "Before we talk about a solution — what do you think the actual problem is?"

A good answer restates your problem back to you in their own words, and asks a follow-up question. A bad answer skips straight to "here's what we'd build," because they were never really listening for the problem — they were listening for a chance to pitch.

2. "Can I see a case study with real detail, not just a screenshot?"

Anyone can show a polished mockup. What you actually want to know: what was the situation before, what changed, and why did they build it that way instead of some other way. If every case study reads like a highlight reel with no specifics, that's the pattern to notice.

3. "Who exactly will work on my project?"

Find out if the person pitching you is the person building it, or if it gets handed to a team you haven't met once signed. Neither answer is automatically wrong — but you should know which one you're getting before you sign, not after.

4. "What happens if the scope needs to change halfway through?"

Scope changes on real projects — that's normal, not a red flag. What matters is whether they have an actual process for it (a change request, a re-estimate, a conversation) or whether it just becomes a source of tension and scope-creep resentment later.

5. "Who owns the code once this is done?"

You should own the code and the intellectual property for anything custom-built for your business, in writing, from day one. If an agency is vague about this or it's not in the contract, ask directly before signing anything.

6. "What does support look like after launch?"

Software doesn't stop needing attention the day it ships — bugs surface under real usage, and businesses change. Find out if post-launch support is a real, defined arrangement or an afterthought they'll figure out if you ask again later.

7. "Can I talk to a past client?"

Not every agency can offer this — some client work is confidential, and that's legitimate. But if an agency has zero references, zero named case studies, and zero willingness to explain why, that's worth asking about directly rather than letting slide.

8. "What would you tell me not to build?"

This is the question that actually reveals whether you're talking to a vendor or a partner. An agency that will only ever say yes to more scope is optimizing for their invoice. One willing to tell you a feature isn't worth building yet is optimizing for your actual outcome.

The pattern underneath all eight

None of these questions are really about technology. They're about whether the agency treats your problem as the starting point or as a formality on the way to a proposal. That's usually decided in the first conversation, before any contract exists — which is exactly why it's worth paying attention to in a free call before you commit to anything.
