A logo is the smallest part of a brand identity

When people say they need "branding," they often mean a logo. A logo matters, but on its own it's a single asset. A brand identity is the system that makes every touchpoint — website, packaging, social posts, invoices — feel like it came from the same business. That system is what builds recognition and trust over time.

What a complete identity covers

1. Strategy: who you are and who you're for

Positioning, audience, tone of voice and what makes you different. This is the least visible part and the most important, because every visual decision afterwards should trace back to it. Skipping it is how brands end up looking polished but saying nothing.

2. The visual system

  • Logo suite — primary mark plus the variations you actually need: horizontal, stacked, icon-only, single-colour.
  • Colour palette — primary and secondary colours with specific values for screen and print.
  • Typography — the typefaces, sizes and hierarchy used across headings and body copy.
  • Imagery and iconography style — how photos, illustrations and icons should look and feel.

3. Brand guidelines

The document that lets anyone — a new hire, a freelancer, a printer — apply the identity correctly without asking. Good guidelines cover spacing, misuse examples, colour ratios and voice, so the brand stays consistent when you're not in the room.

4. Applications

The identity in use: website, packaging, stationery, social templates, signage, presentations. These are where a system either proves itself or falls apart, which is why we design a representative set rather than handing over a logo file and stopping.

Why doing it as one system pays off

Brand, packaging and website designed by three separate vendors tend to drift apart. Building them together keeps decisions consistent, so the packaging colour matches the site and the tone on the label matches the tone in the checkout email. Our Golf Garage case study shows this in practice — identity, packaging and a Shopify storefront developed as one connected system.

Questions to settle before you start

  • Who is the identity for, and what should they feel within the first few seconds?
  • Which applications matter most in the first year — website, packaging, social, print?
  • Does anything from your current brand need to be kept, or is this a clean start?
  • Who will apply the guidelines day to day, and what do they need from them?
